Why a Cash Sale Makes Sense During Divorce

Listing a house during a divorce is a recipe for more conflict:

  • Who pays for the repairs to get it "market ready"?
  • Who coordinates showings with buyers and agents?
  • What if one spouse refuses to leave during showings?
  • Who makes the decision when a low offer comes in?
  • What if it sits on the market for 3-6 months while you're still connected?
Every month the house sits unsold is another month you're financially tied to someone you want to separate from. A cash sale cuts that connection in days, not months.

Florida Divorce & Property Laws: What You Need to Know

Florida is an equitable distribution state. This means marital property is divided fairly — not necessarily 50/50. The marital home is typically considered marital property if it was purchased during the marriage.

Key points:

  • Both spouses usually must agree to sell, unless a court orders it
  • If one spouse wants to keep the house, they typically need to buy out the other's equity — which requires cash or refinancing
  • A cash sale gives both parties immediate liquidity to start fresh
  • We can work with your attorney to ensure the sale meets court requirements

Frequently Asked Questions — Divorce Home Sales

Do both spouses have to agree to sell?

In most cases, yes — if it's marital property. However, if one spouse refuses to cooperate, the other can file for a partition action (court-ordered sale). A cash offer is often the easiest path to agreement because it's fast and eliminates ongoing costs.

How do we split the money?

The title company can wire funds to separate accounts based on your agreement or court order. Typically this is 50/50, but it depends on your specific situation and any prenuptial agreements.

What if one spouse still lives in the house?

No problem. We can schedule closing around their move-out date. We also offer flexible timelines so the occupying spouse has time to find a new place.

Can we sell if we're not officially divorced yet?

Yes. Many couples sell before the divorce is final. The proceeds are held in escrow or disbursed according to a temporary agreement. Your attorney can structure this.

Will you work with our attorneys?

Absolutely. We regularly work with divorce attorneys in Brevard County. We can send the purchase agreement to both attorneys for review and coordinate closing with the legal timeline.

Do we need to make repairs first?

No. We buy as-is. This eliminates one of the biggest arguments during divorce — who pays for repairs to get the house ready for market.
