The Challenges of Selling Rental Properties During Retirement

Selling a rental property is not as simple as selling a primary residence. There are tenants to consider, tax implications to navigate, and property conditions that may require significant investment. Here is what retiring landlords in Cocoa face:

Tenant-Occupied Properties

If your rental still has tenants, traditional buyers may be scared away. Most homebuyers want to move in themselves, not inherit a lease. You may need to wait for leases to expire, offer cash-for-keys, or deal with eviction proceedings in Brevard County courts, which can take months.

Deferred Maintenance

After years of cash flow-focused management, many rental properties have deferred maintenance. Roofs, HVAC systems, plumbing, and electrical work may need major updates before a traditional buyer will make an offer. Spending $20,000 on repairs to get market price does not make sense when you are trying to retire.

Capital Gains and Depreciation Recapture

When you sell a rental property, the IRS wants its cut. You will owe capital gains tax on appreciation, plus depreciation recapture on all the depreciation you claimed over the years. A property you bought for $100,000 that is now worth $300,000 could generate a significant tax bill. However, a 1031 exchange or strategic timing of the sale can help minimize this.

Emotional Attachment

Many landlords bought their first rental decades ago. These properties represent years of hard work, sacrifice, and financial discipline. Letting go can be emotionally difficult, even when selling is the smartest financial move.

Tax Strategies for Retiring Landlords in Florida

Florida has no state income tax, which is great news for retiring landlords. But you still need to plan for federal taxes when selling rental properties:

1031 Exchange

If you want to stay in real estate but hands-off, a 1031 exchange lets you sell your Cocoa rental and buy a like-kind property (such as a Delaware Statutory Trust or passive real estate investment) without paying capital gains taxes immediately.

Installment Sale

With an installment sale, you act as the bank for the buyer and receive payments over time. This spreads your capital gains tax liability across multiple years, potentially keeping you in a lower tax bracket.

Primary Residence Conversion

If you lived in the rental property for 2 of the last 5 years before selling, you may qualify for the Section 121 exclusion: up to $250,000 (single) or $500,000 (married) of capital gains tax-free.

Step-Up in Basis

If you pass rental properties to your heirs, they receive a step-up in basis to the current market value. This can eliminate capital gains tax entirely for your estate. Consult with a Florida estate planning attorney to explore this option.
