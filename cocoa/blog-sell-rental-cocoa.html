How to Sell a Rental Property in Cocoa, FL

A tired landlord's guide to exiting real estate in Brevard County

Home → Blog → Sell Rental Property Cocoa

July 2026 — If you own a rental property in Cocoa, Merritt Island, or anywhere in Brevard County and you are thinking about selling, you are not alone. Florida's landlord landscape has shifted dramatically over the past few years. Rising insurance costs, stricter tenant protections, and property taxes that keep climbing have pushed many local landlords to the exit door.

The question is not whether to sell. It is how to sell in a way that protects your equity, your time, and your sanity.

Why Cocoa Landlords Are Selling in 2026

Brevard County has seen a wave of rental property sales driven by a few key factors:

  • Insurance premiums doubled for many landlords after recent hurricane seasons. Properties east of I-95 in Cocoa Beach and Cape Canaveral have been hit especially hard.
  • Property taxes climbed as home values rose across the Space Coast. Your tax bill may not match your rental income anymore.
  • Tenant turnover costs are rising. Cleaning, repairs, marketing, and vacancy periods eat into cash flow faster than before.
  • Aging properties in older Cocoa neighborhoods like Clearlake and Cocoa Villas need major updates roofs, HVAC, plumbing that many landlords do not want to fund.

If you are feeling the squeeze, you have three main paths forward.

Option 1: List with a Realtor

The traditional route. You hire an agent, make repairs, stage the property, list it on the MLS, and wait for a buyer with financing.

Pros: You might get top dollar if the property is in great shape and the market is hot.

Cons:

  • You pay 5-6% in commissions
  • Buyers request inspections and repairs
  • Closing takes 45-60 days on average
  • If tenants are in place, showings are difficult and buyer pool shrinks
  • You keep paying taxes, insurance, and utilities during the listing period

For a landlord who is already burned out, this path can add months of stress.

Option 2: Sell to Another Investor

You can market the property directly to local investors through Facebook groups, BiggerPockets, or word of mouth.

Pros: No agent commission, faster than MLS.

Cons:

  • You need to vet buyers yourself
  • Many "investors" are wholesalers who reassign your contract for a profit
  • Offers vary wildly
  • No guarantee the deal closes

Option 3: Sell to a Cash Home Buyer

This is the route that tired landlords in Cocoa, Rockledge, and Titusville are choosing when speed and certainty matter more than squeezing out every dollar.

How it works:

  1. You call or submit your property info
  2. A local buyer evaluates the property in person or via video walkthrough
  3. You receive a no-obligation cash offer within 24-48 hours
  4. If you accept, you pick the closing date as soon as 7 days or as far out as you need
  5. You sell as-is, with tenants in place or vacant, no repairs, no cleaning

Pros:

  • Close in 7-14 days
  • No repairs, no showings, no agent fees
  • Tenants can stay through closing
  • Certain closing date no financing contingencies

Cons: The offer will be below full retail value, typically 70-85% of market price depending on condition and rental income.

What About Selling with Tenants in Place?

This is the #1 concern we hear from Cocoa landlords. The good news: you have options.

  • Month-to-month tenants: Give proper notice under Florida law (15 days for month-to-month), then sell vacant.
  • Fixed-lease tenants: Sell the property with the lease intact. Cash buyers who specialize in rentals will honor the lease and take over as the new landlord.
  • Problem tenants: A cash buyer can purchase with the tenants in place and handle the eviction or cash-for-keys negotiation themselves.

At Cocoa Cash Home Buyers, we have bought properties with tenants who were behind on rent, properties with Section 8 leases, and properties where the owner just wanted out. Every situation is different, but there is almost always a solution.

The Bottom Line

Selling a rental property in Cocoa does not have to be a six-month ordeal. If your property is costing you more than it is making, or if you are simply ready to move on, a direct cash sale can give you a clean exit and a check in hand within two weeks.
