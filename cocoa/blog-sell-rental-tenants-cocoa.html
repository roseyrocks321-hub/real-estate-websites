Understanding Florida Landlord-Tenant Law Before You Sell

Before you list your rental property or contact a buyer, you must understand how Florida law protects tenants during a sale. Under Florida Statute Chapter 83, tenants have specific rights that do not disappear simply because the property changes hands. This is one of the most misunderstood aspects of selling tenant-occupied property in Brevard County.

The Lease Survives the Sale

In Florida, a lease agreement is binding on the new owner. This means that if your tenant has six months remaining on a fixed-term lease, the buyer must honor that lease until it expires. You cannot force a tenant to leave simply because you want to sell. This is true whether your property is a single-family home in Cocoa's historic district, a duplex near Rockledge, or a rental unit in Merritt Island.

For month-to-month tenants, Florida law requires you to provide at least 15 days' written notice before the end of the rental period if you wish to terminate the tenancy. However, if you are selling the property, many buyers — especially traditional homebuyers looking for a primary residence — will not want to inherit a month-to-month tenant. This can significantly shrink your buyer pool and extend your time on market.

Tenant Rights During Showings and Inspections

Florida law requires landlords to give reasonable notice before entering a rental unit. While "reasonable" is not strictly defined by statute, 12 to 24 hours is generally accepted as standard. Tenants also have the right to quiet enjoyment of the property, which means you cannot bombard them with daily showings or inspections.

These restrictions make it harder to sell through traditional channels. Realtors in the Cocoa area often report that tenant-occupied properties receive fewer showings, lower offers, and more contingency issues than vacant homes. If your tenant is uncooperative, the selling process can become a nightmare.

Selling with a Fixed-Term Lease vs. Month-to-Month

The type of tenancy agreement you have in place dramatically affects your selling strategy. Understanding the difference is critical for landlords in Cocoa and surrounding Brevard County communities.

Selling with a Fixed-Term Lease

A fixed-term lease — typically 12 months — offers stability but limits flexibility. Your tenant has the right to remain until the lease expires, and any buyer must take the property subject to that lease. Your options include:

  • Waiting for the lease to expire: This is the simplest route but may not work if you need to sell quickly due to financial pressure, a PCS move from Patrick SFB, or personal circumstances.
  • Selling to an investor: Cash buyers and real estate investors often prefer properties with tenants in place because it means immediate rental income. This is especially common in the Cocoa area, where military families, Space Coast workers, and seasonal residents create steady demand for rental housing.
  • Negotiating an early exit: Some tenants may agree to leave early if you offer a financial incentive, commonly called "cash for keys." This can be effective but requires careful documentation and a willing tenant.

Selling with a Month-to-Month Tenancy

Month-to-month tenancies offer more flexibility. Under Florida law, you can terminate the tenancy with at least 15 days' written notice before the end of the monthly period. However, if you are mid-month and need to sell quickly, you may still face a waiting period.

Many landlords in Cocoa, particularly those with older properties near downtown or along US-1, have long-term month-to-month tenants who have been in place for years. While this can make eviction or termination feel morally difficult, remember that your financial wellbeing matters too. If you are burned out, underwater on repairs, or facing a life change, you have every right to exit the rental business.

The Hidden Costs of Selling a Tenant-Occupied Property

Many landlords underestimate the true cost of selling a rental with tenants. Beyond the obvious expenses like realtor commissions and closing costs, there are hidden burdens that can eat away at your bottom line.

Tenant Coordination and Property Showings

Coordinating showings around a tenant's schedule is exhausting. Tenants may refuse access, leave the property in poor condition, or even sabotage showings by complaining loudly about maintenance issues. In a competitive market like Cocoa, where buyers can easily choose a clean, vacant home in Viera or Titusville instead, a difficult tenant can cost you the sale.

Repairs and Deferred Maintenance

Tenant-occupied properties often suffer from deferred maintenance. Issues that a homeowner would fix immediately — a leaky faucet, a failing HVAC system, roof damage from Florida storms — are often ignored by tenants or reported too late. Before selling, you may need to invest thousands in repairs to make the property marketable. For landlords already struggling with cash flow, this can be impossible.

Vacancy Risk and Carrying Costs

If you choose to wait for a lease to expire before selling, you face months of carrying costs with no rental income. Mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance do not stop just because the unit is vacant. In Cocoa's fluctuating rental market, a few months of vacancy can wipe out years of profit.

Can You Evict Tenants Just to Sell in Florida?

This is one of the most common questions we hear from tired landlords in Brevard County. The short answer is: not easily, and not ethically if the tenant is paying rent and abiding by the lease.

Florida allows landlords to terminate month-to-month tenancies with proper notice, but you cannot evict a tenant with a fixed-term lease simply because you want to sell. Eviction is only permitted for specific reasons defined by Florida law, including:

  • Non-payment of rent
  • Material violation of the lease agreement
  • Holdover after the lease expires
  • Destruction of the property

Even when you have legitimate grounds, the Brevard County eviction process can take 30 to 90 days or longer, especially if the tenant contests the case or requests a continuance. Court backlogs in Titusville and Viera can add additional delays. For a landlord who needs to sell quickly, eviction is rarely a practical solution.
