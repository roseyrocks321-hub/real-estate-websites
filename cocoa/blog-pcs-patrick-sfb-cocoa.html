PCS from Patrick SFB: Military Home Sale Guide for Cocoa Families

How to sell fast when orders come down

Home → Blog → PCS Patrick SFB Cocoa

Permanent Change of Station orders are a defining feature of military life. One day you are settled in your Cocoa home, enjoying weekend trips to the Space Coast beaches and community events near Patrick Space Force Base. The next day, orders arrive, and you have months — sometimes weeks — to relocate your entire family across the country or overseas. For military families in Brevard County, the pressure to sell a home quickly before a PCS move is intense, and the financial stakes are high.

Whether you are active duty Space Force, a civilian contractor, or a military spouse, this guide will help you understand your options for selling your Cocoa home fast before PCS. From DITY moves to government relocation programs, from renting to cash sales, we will cover the strategies that work when the clock is ticking and your family is counting on you.

Understanding the PCS Timeline and Housing Decision

PCS timelines vary by branch, rank, and assignment, but most families receive orders 60 to 120 days before their report date. That sounds like plenty of time until you factor in packing, school transitions, spousal employment changes, and the reality of selling a home in Cocoa's competitive but unpredictable market.

The Critical Question: Sell or Rent?

Every military family facing PCS from Patrick SFB must answer one fundamental question: should we sell our home or rent it out? Both options have merit, but the right choice depends on your specific circumstances.

Selling makes sense when:

  • You need the equity for a down payment at your next duty station
  • The Brevard County market favors sellers and you can close quickly
  • You do not want the stress of being a long-distance landlord
  • Your home needs repairs or updates that you cannot complete before moving
  • You cannot afford two mortgages if the property sits vacant

Renting makes sense when:

  • The Cocoa rental market is strong and you have reliable property management
  • You plan to return to Brevard County and want to keep your home
  • The home has appreciated significantly and you want to defer capital gains
  • You have enough savings to cover vacancies and maintenance from afar

For many families, the math favors selling. Property management fees in Brevard County typically run 8% to 12% of monthly rent, vacancies are common during hurricane season, and emergency repairs on a Cocoa home from 2,000 miles away are a nightmare. Unless you have a compelling reason to hold the property, selling before PCS is often the smartest financial and emotional decision.

DITY Move vs. Government Move: Housing Implications

Your choice of moving method can indirectly affect your home sale timeline. The military offers two primary relocation options: a government-arranged move (where a contracted moving company packs and transports your belongings) or a Do-It-Yourself (DITY) move (where you handle the move and receive reimbursement based on weight).

Government-Pro Move (Full-Service)

A government move requires less effort but offers less flexibility. The moving company schedules packing and pickup dates, and you must be ready on their timeline. If your home sale falls through and you need to extend your Cocoa stay, you may face storage fees or complex rescheduling. For families selling on a tight timeline, the rigidity of a government move can add stress.

DITY Move (Personally Procured Move)

A DITY move gives you control over timing, which can be invaluable when coordinating a home sale. You can pack at your own pace, schedule movers around closing dates, and potentially pocket extra money if your costs come in under the military's reimbursement rate. However, DITY moves require significant planning and physical effort, which can be overwhelming while simultaneously managing a home sale.

Many Cocoa military families choose a partial DITY move, handling high-value items themselves while letting the government move the bulk of their household goods. This hybrid approach offers flexibility without requiring you to rent a 26-foot truck and drive it across the country with kids in tow.

SCRA Protections and Your Home Sale

The Servicemembers Civil Relief Act (SCRA) provides important protections for active-duty military members, and understanding these protections can help you make better decisions about your home sale.

Mortgage Interest Rate Reduction

Under SCRA, active-duty servicemembers can cap mortgage interest rates at 6% for debts incurred before entering active duty. If you purchased your Cocoa home before your current active-duty period and your interest rate is above 6%, you may be entitled to a reduction. This can lower your monthly payments while you prepare to sell, reducing financial pressure during the PCS transition.

Foreclosure Protection

SCRA protects active-duty members from foreclosure without a court order. While no military family wants to face foreclosure, this protection provides a safety net if your home sale takes longer than expected or if you encounter financial hardship during the transition. Lenders must obtain a court order to foreclose on a property owned by an active-duty servicemember, giving you additional time and legal leverage.

Tax Implications of SCRA

SCRA also affects your state of legal residence for tax purposes. Many military families stationed at Patrick SFB maintain their original home state for tax purposes, which can complicate capital gains calculations when selling a Cocoa property. Consult a tax professional familiar with military relocations to ensure you understand your obligations.

The Challenge of Selling on the Traditional Market Before PCS

Selling a home through traditional channels — listing with a realtor, staging, showings, inspections, and buyer financing — takes time. In Brevard County, the average days on market ranges from 30 to 60 days, depending on price point and neighborhood. Add 30 to 45 days for buyer financing and closing, and you are looking at 60 to 105 days from listing to cash in hand.

For a military family with PCS orders in hand, that timeline is risky. If your buyer's financing falls through, if the appraisal comes in low, or if inspection issues derail the contract, you could be left with an empty house in Cocoa while paying for housing at your new duty station.

Common Obstacles for Military Sellers in Cocoa

  • Appraisal gaps: Lenders require appraisals, and if the appraiser undervalues your Cocoa home compared to similar sales in Rockledge or Titusville, the buyer may demand a price reduction or walk away entirely.
  • Inspection failures: Older homes near Patrick SFB often have issues with roofs, HVAC systems, or Florida-specific concerns like termite damage. Buyers may request costly repairs or credits that delay closing.
  • Buyers with contingency clauses: Many buyers in Brevard County must sell their current home before purchasing yours. If their sale falls through, so does yours.
  • Seasonal market fluctuations: The Cocoa market slows during summer and hurricane season. If your PCS coincides with these periods, your home may sit longer than expected.

PCS Checklist for Selling Your Cocoa Home

If you are preparing to sell your home before PCS, use this checklist to stay organized and avoid last-minute surprises:

  1. Gather your documents: Collect your deed, mortgage statement, homeowners insurance policy, property tax records, HOA documents, and any repair receipts.
  2. Notify your lender: Inform your mortgage servicer of your PCS orders and anticipated sale timeline. Ask about any prepayment penalties or SCRA benefits.
  3. Contact the housing office: Patrick SFB's housing office can provide resources, referrals, and guidance for transitioning families.
  4. Request your VA certificate of eligibility: If you used a VA loan, request your COE to facilitate assumption or payoff.
  5. Get multiple offers: Whether you list traditionally or seek cash buyers, compare your options. Do not assume the first offer is the best.
  6. Plan for temporary housing: If your home sells before your report date, arrange temporary lodging or base housing at your new duty station.
  7. Update your address: File a change of address with USPS, update DEERS, and notify your bank, insurance, and subscriptions.

Local Market Insights for Patrick SFB Families

Cocoa and the surrounding Brevard County communities have a unique housing market shaped by the presence of Patrick Space Force Base, the Space Coast's aerospace industry, and Florida's seasonal population. Understanding these local factors can help you price and market your home effectively.

Homes within a 20-minute commute of Patrick SFB tend to sell faster than those farther inland, particularly among military buyers who value proximity to base. Neighborhoods like Cocoa Isles, Rockledge near Barton Boulevard, and Merritt Island near the causeway are perennial favorites among Space Coast military families.

However, the market also has challenges. Flood zones near the Indian River and Banana River can increase insurance costs and complicate sales. Older homes built before 1990 may need wind mitigation inspections to qualify for insurance discounts. Cash buyers who know the local market understand these nuances and factor them into their offers, saving you the headache of addressing them yourself.
