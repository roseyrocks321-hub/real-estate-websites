The PCS Timeline Challenge for Patrick SFB Families

PCS orders typically come with tight deadlines. You may have 30 to 60 days to pack, find housing at your next duty station, enroll kids in new schools, and figure out what to do with your current property. For families living in Cocoa, Cocoa Beach, Merritt Island, or nearby Space Coast communities, the pressure is even greater because the local real estate market can be unpredictable.

According to recent Brevard County housing data, the average days on market for a traditional home sale is hovering between 45 and 75 days depending on price point and neighborhood. That does not include the time spent preparing the home for listing, negotiating repairs after inspection, or waiting for buyer financing to clear. For a military family with hard orders and a report date, 45 days might as well be a lifetime.

That is where selling directly to a cash home buyer becomes a game-changer.

Why the Traditional MLS Route Fails During PCS Moves

Listing your home with a realtor is the default option for many homeowners, and it can work well in stable situations. But during a PCS, the traditional sale process introduces risks that most military families cannot afford:

  • Buyer financing falls through. Even pre-approved buyers can lose loan approval due to credit changes, job shifts, or lender issues. When you are counting on a closing date to align with your move, a financing failure can derail everything.
  • Inspections trigger repair demands. Most buyers request repairs after inspection. If you are already at your new duty station, coordinating contractors from hundreds of miles away is nearly impossible.
  • Appraisal gaps kill deals. If the home appraises below the agreed price, the buyer may walk or demand a price reduction. You are left re-listing while paying two mortgages.
  • Showings are disruptive. Keeping the house showing-ready while packing for a move is exhausting. If you have already relocated, you are relying on neighbors, agents, or property managers to handle access.
  • You pay carrying costs the entire time. Every month the house sits unsold, you are paying the mortgage, property taxes, homeowners insurance, utilities, and lawn maintenance. Over 60 days, that can easily exceed $3,000 to $5,000.

For families PCSing from Patrick SFB, these risks are not hypothetical. They happen regularly, and they add enormous stress to an already demanding transition.

What About My VA Loan or Housing Allowance?

Military homeowners often worry about how a fast sale affects their VA loan entitlement or Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH). The short answer: it does not create problems. Selling your home releases your VA entitlement, making it available for your next purchase. Your BAH at the new duty station is based on your new zip code, not your old one.

If you are concerned about capital gains, most military families qualify for exclusions. If you lived in the home as your primary residence for at least two of the last five years, you can exclude up to $250,000 of gain (or $500,000 if married filing jointly). Given that many Patrick SFB families have owned homes for several years, this usually eliminates any tax burden entirely.

Is a Cash Offer Lower Than a Traditional Sale?

Yes, a cash offer is typically below full retail market value. Cash buyers in Cocoa usually pay 70% to 85% of the home's after-repair value, depending on condition and location. But before you dismiss the option, run the numbers on a traditional sale:

  • Realtor commissions: 5% to 6% of sale price
  • Buyer-requested repairs: $2,000 to $10,000+
  • Closing costs and concessions: 1% to 3%
  • Carrying costs during 45-75 days on market: $3,000 to $6,000
  • Staging, cleaning, and prep: $500 to $2,000
  • Stress and uncertainty: impossible to quantify

When you subtract these expenses from a full-price MLS sale, the net proceeds are often surprisingly close to a cash offer. And for a family facing a hard PCS deadline, the certainty and speed of a cash sale is worth far more than a theoretical top dollar.

Local Market Realities in Cocoa and Brevard County

Cocoa's housing market is diverse. Homes near the historic downtown or along the Indian River tend to move faster than properties in older neighborhoods needing significant updates. But regardless of where you live in Brevard County, buyer demand fluctuates seasonally. Summer months see a spike in activity, but fall and winter can slow dramatically as snowbirds wait for cooler weather.

For a military family with a January or February PCS, listing in the slow season can mean 90+ days on market. A cash buyer eliminates seasonal risk entirely. They purchase year-round because they are not dependent on retail buyer demand.
